# Words and Statuses You'll See

Peekaboo has a vocabulary of its own. Some of it is ordinary early-years language, some of
it is finance language, and a little of it is Peekaboo's own warm naming — children are
**Our Loved Ones** and staff are **Our Heroes** in one or two places.

This guide has two parts.

**Part A** is a glossary of every term you will meet, in alphabetical order. For each one:
what it means in plain words, the everyday synonyms colleagues will use, and where on
screen you will see it.

**Part B** lists every status and label the portal shows you, grouped by area, with what
each means and what normally happens next. It finishes with a one-line lookup table you can
scan in a hurry.

Bold text is exactly what appears on your screen.

---

# Part A — Glossary

**Absent** — An attendance status meaning the person did not attend and the absence was not
authorised in advance. Available for both children and staff.
*Also called:* away, off, no-show, did not attend.
*Where you see it:* Children Attendance; Staff Attendance; Attendance Reports.

**Accepted** — The admissions stage meaning your nursery has agreed to offer the child a
place. Acceptance is not the same as enrolment — the child record still has to be created.
*Also called:* approved, offered a place, successful.
*Where you see it:* Leads.

**Active** — A record that is in use. Children, staff, classes, curricula, learning areas,
sessions and roles all carry this setting, and all of them start as Active.
*Also called:* live, current, in use, enabled, on the books.
*Where you see it:* Children; Staff; Classes; Curriculum; Learn Area; My Center; Roles &
Access.

**Admission** — The admissions stage meaning the family is proceeding into the application
process proper, with paperwork to complete.
*Also called:* application, applying, in process.
*Where you see it:* Leads.

**Admissions** — The area of the portal covering parent enquiries about nursery places,
from first contact through a tour to enrolment. Holds **Leads** and **Referrals & Sources**.
*Also called:* CRM, enrolments, the enquiry list, the waiting list.
*Where you see it:* sidebar group.

**Age Group** — An age band such as Playgroup, Nursery or KG1, defined by an age range in
months and belonging to one curriculum. Every class must have one.
*Also called:* age band, year group, cohort, level, room band.
*Where you see it:* Age Groups; Classes; Children filters; Leads.

**Aspect** — A narrower strand inside a learning area. Aspects are what contain the
objectives. On the printed assessment report an aspect may be called a *sub-domain*.
*Also called:* sub-domain, sub-area, strand, sub-topic.
*Where you see it:* Learn Area; the objective filters on Observations.

**Assessment** — Used two ways. Loosely, the whole business of observing children and
reporting on their progress. Specifically, the **Assessments** landing page with its
summary counters.
*Also called:* evaluation, progress tracking, monitoring.
*Where you see it:* Assessments.

**Assessment Report** — The printable summary of one child's progress in one learning area
over a date range, built from the observations recorded in that period. This is what parents
receive at the end of a term.
*Also called:* progress report, end of term report, parent report, learning summary.
*Where you see it:* Assessment Report.

**Attainment** — A level on your nursery's own grading scale, showing how securely a child
has grasped an objective. Each level has a name, a mark, a colour and a description. There
is no built-in scale — your setting creates its own.
*Also called:* achievement level, grade, rating, level, band, standard.
*Where you see it:* Attainment; the rating dropdowns on Observations.

**Attendance Reports** — The read-only history of attendance across a date range, for
children or for staff. The only place to look up a past date.
*Also called:* attendance history, register history, absence report, the archive.
*Where you see it:* Attendance Reports.

**Balance by Class** — The outstanding-fees report, showing what has been billed and what
has been received for each child, and the difference. The portal's answer to "who owes us
money?".
*Also called:* arrears, outstanding fees, debtors, the aged debt list, fee balances.
*Where you see it:* Balance by Class.

**Billed** — On Balance by Class, the total of everything invoiced for a child. Shown in
green with a `+`.
*Also called:* charged, invoiced, raised.
*Where you see it:* Balance by Class.

**Book a Tour** — The admissions stage meaning a nursery visit is being arranged for the
family. The visit has not happened yet.
*Also called:* visit booked, show-round, viewing, open day.
*Where you see it:* Leads.

**Branches** — A field label on the calendar event dialog. Despite the name, the buttons
beneath it are your **classes**, and you pick one at a time. Leave all unselected for a
nursery-wide event.
*Also called:* classes, rooms.
*Where you see it:* Calendar, in the add or edit event dialog.

**Calendar** — The nursery diary of holidays, trips, performances, meetings and key dates,
optionally tagged to a single class.
*Also called:* events, diary, what's on, the planner.
*Where you see it:* Calendar.

**Centers** — The multi-nursery screen listing every nursery on the platform, used to set a
new one up. Only the Principal can open it. **Not** where you edit your own nursery's
details.
*Also called:* branches, sites, campuses, the group view.
*Where you see it:* Centers.

**Centre / Center** — One nursery. Everything in your portal belongs to your centre. You see
only your own.
*Also called:* nursery, setting, school, branch, site, campus.
*Where you see it:* throughout; your own settings live on My Center.

**Check in** — Recording that a person has arrived, which stamps an arrival time. Used for
children on the register and for staff on the clock-in board.
*Also called:* sign in, clock in, arrival, register in.
*Where you see it:* Children Attendance; Staff Attendance.

**Checkout** — Recording that a person has left for the day, which stamps a departure time.
*Also called:* check out, sign out, clock out, home time, collection.
*Where you see it:* Children Attendance; Staff Attendance.

**Child** — A child on your nursery's books, and the central record in the portal.
Attendance, daily reports, health details, pickup people, observations, invoices and
receipts all attach to the child record.
*Also called:* kid, student, pupil, little one, our loved one, key child.
*Where you see it:* Children.

**Children Attendance** — The page for marking **children** present, absent, late or
excused, for today only. A different page from Staff Attendance, with different options.
*Also called:* the register, taking the register, roll call, check-in, the morning register.
*Where you see it:* Children Attendance.

**Class** — A real room at your nursery. Belongs to one age group, can have several teachers
assigned, and carries its own daily-report settings.
*Also called:* classroom, room, group, base room.
*Where you see it:* Classes.

**Comment** — On a daily report, the free-text box for activities and behaviour. On an
assessment report, the written text you add for each child.
*Also called:* notes, remarks, write-up.
*Where you see it:* Daily Reports; Assessment Report.

**CRM** — Here, **parents enquiring about a nursery place**. This is the point worth being
clear about: Peekaboo's CRM is admissions, not sales. There are no deals, no quotes, no
revenue pipeline and no sales targets. When someone says "check the CRM", they mean the
enquiry list.
*Also called:* admissions, leads, enquiries, the waiting list, the pipeline.
*Where you see it:* Leads; the CRM tab of the dashboard.

**Curriculum** — The early-years framework your nursery assesses against, and the top of the
whole learning structure. It owns your age groups and your attainment scale, and contains
the learning areas.
*Also called:* framework, scheme of work, the curriculum model.
*Where you see it:* Curriculum.

**Daily Care** — The section of a daily report holding water, juice, medicine and hygiene as
free-text boxes.
*Also called:* care notes, personal care.
*Where you see it:* Daily Reports.

**Daily Report** — The care record for one child on one day that goes home to the family:
mood, meals, nap, milk, items needed, and the note to parents. Saved as a draft, then
published, and emailed to the parent.
*Also called:* journal, day sheet, care report, diary, daily sheet, what they did today.
*Where you see it:* Daily Reports.

**Dashboard** — See **Live Dashboard**.

**Dietary** — A type of health record used for food restrictions, intolerances and special
diets. This is where special diets belong; there is no separate dietary screen.
*Also called:* special diet, food requirement, intolerance, food plan.
*Where you see it:* Children, in the Health section of a child's record.

**Draft** — A daily report that has been saved but not yet published, so no parent can see
it.
*Also called:* unpublished, work in progress, not sent yet.
*Where you see it:* Daily Reports.

**Early Childhood** — The sidebar group covering classes, curriculum, age groups, attainment,
learning areas, assessments, observations and reports.
*Also called:* curriculum module, learning, EC, the assessment side.
*Where you see it:* sidebar group.

**Early years** — The industry term for childcare and education from birth to around five —
the whole world Peekaboo is built for.
*Also called:* preschool, foundation stage, under-fives.

**Enquiry** — A family's first contact about a place. Stored as a lead.
*Also called:* lead, prospect, enquiry, application, the family who rang.
*Where you see it:* Leads.

**Evaluation** — An admissions stage between application and acceptance, covering assessing
the child's needs or readiness and issuing the offer.
*Also called:* assessment stage, offer letter, review.
*Where you see it:* Leads.

**Excused** — An attendance status for **children only**, meaning an authorised absence — a
planned holiday, a medical appointment, a family event. The staff equivalent is **On Leave**.
*Also called:* authorised absence, approved absence, holiday, planned absence.
*Where you see it:* Children Attendance; Attendance Reports.

**Expense** — A cost the nursery has incurred, linked to a supplier and an expense type, with
the receipt or invoice scan attached. The one page in Money Out built around proof of spend.
*Also called:* cost, spend, outgoing, bill, disbursement.
*Where you see it:* Expenses.

**Expense Type** — A spending category such as Cleaning, Utilities or Stationery. At least
one must exist before an expense can be saved. On the Expenses table this appears under the
column heading **Supply**.
*Also called:* cost category, expense category, spending category, nominal code.
*Where you see it:* Expense Types; Expenses.

**Grade** — On an admissions enquiry, the class or level the family is asking about.
*Also called:* year, level, class, age band.
*Where you see it:* Leads.

**Health record** — A structured entry on a child's record describing a medical matter, with
a type such as Allergy, Chronic or Dietary. Recording an allergy this way is what produces
the red allergy warning on the register.
*Also called:* medical record, allergy record, condition, medical note.
*Where you see it:* Children, in the Health section of a child's record.

**Health Status** — A set of buttons on the daily-report form used while filling it in. See
the note under **Note to parents** — anything about a child's health belongs in the note
box and in a conversation with your room lead.
*Also called:* wellbeing, how they were.
*Where you see it:* Daily Reports.

**Hygiene** — A free-text box in the Daily Care section of a daily report.
*Also called:* toileting, nappy notes, personal care.
*Where you see it:* Daily Reports.

**Inactive** — A record kept for its history but no longer in everyday use. This is
Peekaboo's version of archiving, and it is the right way to handle a child who has left or an
employee who has departed. It can always be switched back. On My Center's sessions table the
same idea reads **Not Active**.
*Also called:* archived, closed, left, former, disabled, retired.
*Where you see it:* Children; Staff; Classes; Curriculum; Learn Area; My Center; Roles &
Access.

**INV** — The sky-blue badge on every invoice card. It is a document label meaning "this is
an invoice", not a status, and it carries no payment information.
*Also called:* the invoice tag.
*Where you see it:* Invoices.

**Invoice** — A bill raised against a **child** for one or more services. Invoices have no
paid or unpaid marker; to see where a family stands, use Balance by Class.
*Also called:* bill, fee note, charge, statement, fee invoice.
*Where you see it:* Invoices.

**Items Needed** — The daily-report section with yes/no toggles for **Diapers**, **Clothes**,
**Wipes** and **Cream**, telling the parent what to send in.
*Also called:* supplies needed, please send, restock request.
*Where you see it:* Daily Reports.

**Late** — An attendance status meaning the person attended but arrived after the expected
start. Available for both children and staff. On the dashboard a late child still counts as
present.
*Also called:* late arrival, tardy, delayed.
*Where you see it:* Children Attendance; Staff Attendance; Attendance Reports; Live
Dashboard.

**Lead** — One prospective family's enquiry, held as a single record carrying both the
child's and the parent's details. A lead is not connected to a child record — when a family
joins, the child is created separately.
*Also called:* enquiry, prospect, applicant, application, potential family.
*Where you see it:* Leads.

**Learn Area / Learning Area** — A broad area of learning within a curriculum, containing
aspects. On the printed assessment report a learning area may be called a *main domain*.
*Also called:* area of learning, domain, main domain, subject, strand.
*Where you see it:* Learn Area; Assessment Report.

**Live Dashboard** — The page you land on after signing in. Three tabs of read-only figures
and charts: **Main Dashboard**, **Finance** and **CRM**. The only page that refreshes itself.
*Also called:* dashboard, home, overview, the front page.
*Where you see it:* Live Dashboard.

**Lost** — The admissions stage meaning the family will not be joining because **they**
withdrew, went elsewhere or went quiet. Distinct from Rejected, where the nursery declined.
*Also called:* dropped, gone cold, withdrawn, not proceeding.
*Where you see it:* Leads.

**Media** — A message type for sending photos to families.
*Also called:* photos, pictures, gallery.
*Where you see it:* Messages.

**Messages** — The one-way noticeboard publishing announcements, newsletters, photos and
videos to parents through the separate parent app. No inbox, no replies, no read receipts.
*Also called:* announcements, newsletter, broadcast, parent comms, the timeline, the
newsfeed.
*Where you see it:* Messages.

**Mood** — The single overall impression of a child's day, chosen under the heading **Today
I Was** on the daily report.
*Also called:* how they were, temperament, spirits.
*Where you see it:* Daily Reports.

**My Center** — Your **own** nursery's settings: name, address, phone numbers, email, logo
and session times. This is where you change your nursery's details — never Centers.
*Also called:* our details, nursery settings, site settings, our profile.
*Where you see it:* My Center.

**Nap** — The daily-report section recording whether and how long a child slept, with
**From** and **To** times.
*Also called:* sleep, rest, quiet time, siesta.
*Where you see it:* Daily Reports.

**Nappy** — Recorded through the **Diapers** toggle in Items Needed and the **Hygiene**
free-text box. There is no separate structured nappy log.
*Also called:* diaper, toileting, changing, bathroom, potty.
*Where you see it:* Daily Reports.

**New Lead** — The admissions stage for a brand-new enquiry that nobody has worked yet. The
top of the queue.
*Also called:* new enquiry, fresh lead, uncontacted.
*Where you see it:* Leads.

**NOS** — The small grey label that sits above most page titles, and the version badge in

*Where you see it:* sidebar header; above most page titles.

*Also called:* inactive, switched off, not offered.
*Where you see it:* My Center.

**Not Marked** — The state of a person whose attendance has not been recorded yet today. It

**Note to parents** — The free-text box on the daily report where you write directly to the

# Part B — Statuses

Every status and label the portal shows you, grouped by where you meet it.

For each one: what it means, when it appears, what normally happens next, and whether you
need to do anything.

---

## Children's attendance

Marked on **Children Attendance**, for today only. Remember these four are for children —
staff have a slightly different set.

### Present
**What it means:** The child is in the nursery today and has been checked in.
**When it appears:** When you click **Present** on the child's row, or choose it in **Edit**.
**Colour:** Green.
**What happens next:** An arrival time is stamped automatically if one was not already there.
At the end of the day a **Checkout** button appears on the row so you can record their
departure.
**Do you need to do anything?** **Yes — click it as each child arrives.** Nothing marks a
child present on its own.

### Absent
**What it means:** The child did not attend today, and the absence was not arranged in
advance.
**When it appears:** When you click **Absent**, or choose it in **Edit**.
**Colour:** Red.
**What happens next:** Both the arrival and departure times are cleared, which is correct —
the child was not here.
**Do you need to do anything?** **Yes — record why in the Notes box inside Edit.** There is
no reason dropdown, so the note is the record. If you later switch the status back, re-enter
the times by hand, since they were cleared.

### Late
**What it means:** The child is here, but arrived after the expected start time.
**When it appears:** When you click **Late**.
**Colour:** Amber.
**What happens next:** An arrival time is stamped if one was not already there. The child
appears in the **Late Arrivals** panel on the **Live Dashboard**, with your note shown as the
reason — or **No reason given** if you left the note blank. A late child counts as present in
the dashboard's **Children Present** figure, since they are in the building.
**Do you need to do anything?** **Yes — put the reason in Notes**, so the dashboard shows
something useful.

### Excused
**What it means:** The child is away and the absence was authorised — a planned holiday, a
medical appointment, a family event.
**When it appears:** When you click **Excused**.
**Colour:** Blue.
**What happens next:** Arrival and departure times are cleared, as with Absent. On Attendance
Reports, excused days are included in the **Total Absent Days** figure alongside absent and
leave days.
**Do you need to do anything?** **Yes — note the authorisation in Notes**, so the record
shows the absence was agreed.
**Note:** Excused is for **children only**. For staff, use **On Leave**.

### Not Marked
**What it means:** Nobody has recorded anything for this child today. **It does not mean
absent.**
**When it appears:** Automatically, on every child's row at the start of each day, until
somebody marks them.
**Colour:** Grey.
**What happens next:** Nothing at all, unless a person acts. No reminder is sent and nothing
runs overnight.
**Do you need to do anything?** **Yes — this is the most important action on the page.**
Unmarked children are counted in neither the present nor the absent totals, which is why
dashboard figures sometimes do not add up to the full roll. Clearing every **Not Marked**
before the morning is out is the single best habit in this module.
**Note:** Once a status has been saved there is no way to return a child to Not Marked. Pick
the status that fits rather than trying to undo.

## Staff attendance

Marked on **Staff Attendance**, for today only.

### Present
**What it means:** The employee has clocked in today.
**When it appears:** When you click **Check in**, or choose Present in **Edit**.
**Colour:** Green.
**What happens next:** **Check in** is replaced by **Checkout**. Pressing Checkout at the end
of the shift stamps the departure time and keeps the status as it was.
**Do you need to do anything?** **Yes — check each employee in.**
**Note:** Once an arrival time exists the **Check in** button does not come back. To adjust
the time, use **Edit**.

### Absent
**What it means:** The employee did not attend, and was not on authorised leave.
**When it appears:** When you click **Absent**, or choose it in **Edit**.
**Colour:** Red.
**What happens next:** The quick button clears both times. The edit dialog leaves the times
as you have set them, so if you want a clean absent record the quick button is the tidier
route.
**Do you need to do anything?** Yes, mark it. There is no leave-request link and no approval
step here — this is a record of the day, not a request.

### Late
**What it means:** The employee attended but arrived after their scheduled start.
**When it appears:** **Only from the Edit dialog.** There is no quick button for Late on this
page — open **Edit**, choose **Late** from the status dropdown, and save.
**Colour:** Amber.
**What happens next:** Nothing automatic. The portal does not accumulate lateness totals or
calculate hours worked.
**Do you need to do anything?** Yes, and note the extra step: staff lateness takes two clicks
where a child's takes one.

### On Leave
**What it means:** The employee is away on authorised leave — holiday, approved time off,
sickness leave.
**When it appears:** When you click the quick button reading **Leave**, or choose **On Leave**
in **Edit**. The summary card reads **On Leave**. All three refer to the same thing.
**Colour:** Blue.
**What happens next:** The quick button clears both times. On Attendance Reports, leave days
are included in the **Total Absent Days** figure alongside absent and excused days.
**Do you need to do anything?** Yes, mark it. Approving leave itself happens through your own
process outside the portal.
**Note:** On Leave is for **staff only**. For children, use **Excused**.

### Not Marked
**What it means:** Nothing has been recorded for this employee today.
**When it appears:** Automatically each day until somebody marks them.
**Colour:** Grey.
**What happens next:** Nothing.
**Do you need to do anything?** Yes — clock them in, or mark them Absent or Leave.
**Note:** If you open **Edit** on an unmarked row, the status dropdown starts at **Present**
as a helpful default. Change it if that is not what you mean.

### P
**What it means:** Present that day. Hovering shows **Present**.
**When it appears:** When present was recorded for that person and date.
**Colour:** Solid green circle.
**What happens next:** Counts towards **Total Present Days** and the present percentage.
**Do you need to do anything?** No.

### A
**What it means:** Absent that day. Hovering shows **Absent**.

**What it means:** Arrived late that day. Hovering shows **Late**.
**When it appears:** When late was recorded.
**Colour:** Amber outline, pale amber fill.

## Active and Inactive

The same pair appears across the portal — on children, staff, classes, curricula, learning
areas, sessions and roles.

### Active
**What it means:** The record is in use and appears in dropdowns and default lists.
**When it appears:** On creation. **Everything starts Active.**
**Colour:** Green in most places.
**What happens next:** The record shows up wherever it is needed — active classes appear in
the class dropdown when registering a child, active sessions appear in the timing dropdown,
active classes appear as filter chips on Balance by Class.
**Do you need to do anything?** No. Active is the default and is usually what you want.

### Inactive
**What it means:** The record is kept for its history but should no longer be used in
everyday work. **This is Peekaboo's version of archiving.**
**When it appears:** When somebody deliberately switches the status off.
**Colour:** Usually red or grey, depending on the page. On **Curriculum** and **Learn Area**
the badge displays in green whatever the setting, so on those two pages **read the word
rather than the colour**.
**What happens next:** The record disappears from dropdowns where a live choice is needed —
classes, sessions — but stays in list views unless you filter it out. Nothing is deleted.
**Do you need to do anything?** Yes, deliberately: **this is the right way to handle a child
who has left or an employee who has departed.** It keeps their history intact and can be
switched back at any time.
**Two practical notes:** On the **Children** page, the **Clear** button resets search, class
and age filters but leaves the status filter where you set it — set it back to **All Status**
by hand. And on **Staff** and **Roles**, the one-click status button flips immediately with
no confirmation, so click it deliberately.

### Not Active
**What it means:** Exactly the same as Inactive — the wording just differs on this one table.
**When it appears:** On the sessions table on **My Center**.
**Colour:** Grey.
**What happens next:** The session disappears from the timing dropdown when registering a
child.
**Do you need to do anything?** Yes if somebody reports an empty timing dropdown while
registering a child — that is the usual cause. Switch a session back on, or create one.

## Admissions enquiry stages

These appear on **Leads** as the coloured badge on each row. Below they are listed in the
order a family actually travels through them.

### Where to look for the authoritative stage

Before the list, one point that will save you real confusion.

**The badge on the Leads page is the authoritative stage.** Some stages appear under
different wording on the **Live Dashboard**, and under slightly different wording again in the
menu you use to move an enquiry along. This shows up most around the tour stages, where the
dashboard and the Leads page use different words for the same point in the journey.

Two practical consequences:

- **When you need to know where one family stands, read the badge on the Leads page.** Use the
  dashboard for the shape of the month — are enquiries up, are we converting — and the Leads
  page for the individual answer.
- **If you move an enquiry using a menu option and the badge afterwards reads differently,
  the enquiry has moved correctly.** The stage is right; the two screens simply name it
  differently. Nothing has gone wrong and you do not need to redo it.

Also worth knowing: **read the stage by its word, not its colour.** Only one stage is red, and
two rather different outcomes share a similar blue, so the badge text is the reliable signal.

### New Lead
**What it means:** A brand-new enquiry that nobody has worked yet.
**When it appears:** When an enquiry is added, and as the starting point for any enquiry whose
stage is not recognised.
**Colour:** Blue.
**What happens next:** Somebody calls or emails the family and arranges a visit, moving the
enquiry to **Book a Tour**.
**Do you need to do anything?** **Yes.** This is the top of the queue — an enquiry sitting
here has had no contact at all.

### Book a Tour
**What it means:** A nursery visit is being arranged for the family. **The visit has not
happened yet.**
**When it appears:** When you agree a visit with the parent and set this stage.
**Colour:** Amber.
**What happens next:** After the visit, the enquiry moves on to **KYC** and then through the
application stages.
**Do you need to do anything?** **Yes.** Amber means an appointment is pending. Capture the
date and time in the follow-up task — there is no separate tour booking screen, so the task is
where the details live.

### KYC
**What it means:** "Know Your Customer" — the information-gathering step, and in most
nurseries' use the point at which the family has visited and details are being collected and
checked.
**When it appears:** When you set this stage, generally after the tour.
**Colour:** Blue.
**What happens next:** The enquiry moves on to **Admission**, or to **Evaluation**.
**Do you need to do anything?** **Yes — and agree with your team what this stage means for
you.** Nurseries use it slightly differently. Write your own definition down so everyone reads
the badge the same way.

### Evaluation
**What it means:** The assessment stage — looking at the child's needs or readiness, and the
point at which an offer is issued.
**When it appears:** When you set this stage.
**Colour:** Blue.
**What happens next:** The family moves on to **Accepted**, or the enquiry closes as
**Rejected** or **Lost**.
**Do you need to do anything?** Yes — whatever your evaluation process requires.

### Admission
**What it means:** The family is proceeding into the admission process proper — the
application stage, with paperwork to complete.
**When it appears:** When the family decides to apply.
**Colour:** Green.
**What happens next:** Evaluation, then acceptance.
**Do you need to do anything?** **Yes** — the admission paperwork needs chasing.

### Accepted
**What it means:** Your nursery has accepted the child.
**When it appears:** When you set this stage, or when you press the **Approve** button in the
enquiry's detail header.
**Colour:** Green.
**What happens next:** The family completes registration, and ideally the enquiry reaches
**Registered**.
**Do you need to do anything?** **Yes, and this is the most important action item in the whole
module. Acceptance is not enrolment.** No child record is created by this stage. Open the
**Children** page and create the child properly — name, birth date, parent, health details,
pickup people, and the registration that puts them in a room. A good standing rule is: *when a
lead reaches Accepted, create the child record the same day.*
**Note:** Once an enquiry is Accepted, the **Approve** button reads **Approved** and locks.
Moving the stage from the badge menu unlocks it again.

### Registered
**What it means:** The child is registered — the end of the happy path within admissions.
**When it appears:** **Set from the task dialog rather than the badge menu.** Open the
enquiry, press **+** in the Tasks column, and set the stage there.
**Colour:** Green.
**What happens next:** Nothing further within admissions. The enquiry stays in the list as the
historical record of how the family joined.
**Do you need to do anything?** **Yes, outside admissions** — if you have not already created
the child record on the **Children** page, do it now.

### Lost
**What it means:** The family will not be joining, because **they** withdrew, went elsewhere or
went quiet.
**When it appears:** When you set this stage.
**Colour:** Red. This is the only red stage.
**What happens next:** Nothing. The enquiry stays fully visible in the list.
**Do you need to do anything?** Not strictly, but **record why** — in the comment box when you
change the stage, or as a **Note**. Six months on, the reason is the valuable part.

### Rejected
**What it means:** **The nursery** turned the family down, as opposed to Lost, where the family
withdrew.
**When it appears:** When an admissions decision goes against the applicant.
**Colour:** Blue — similar to **New Lead**, so read the word rather than the colour.
**What happens next:** Nothing. The enquiry stays in the list.
**Do you need to do anything?** No, though a **Note** explaining the decision is good practice.

**Two general notes on stages.** Any stage can be set at any time — there is no locked sequence,
so you can correct a mistake by simply setting the right one. And changing a stage creates a
follow-up task and asks for a reminder date; treat that as your prompt to decide when you will
next contact the family, and keep your own diary, because nothing in the portal will chase you
on the day.

**When it appears:** When you press **Publish** or **Publish & Email**.
**Colour:** Green.
**What happens next:** **Publishing on its own does not email anybody.** This is the single
most useful thing to know about the page.
**Do you need to do anything?** **Yes, if the family is to receive it.** Use **Publish &

**When it appears:** When you press **Save Draft**.

**What it means:** No daily report has been written for this child on this date.
**When it appears:** As the starting state for every child each day.
**Colour:** Red.
**What happens next:** Nothing. The envelope button on the card stays unavailable, with the

## Daily report — mood

Chosen under the heading **Today I Was**. Five buttons, and not required — though it is worth
picking one.

| Label | What it means |
|---|---|
| **Great** | A really good day |
| **Good** | A settled, positive day |
| **Okay** | A steady day, nothing remarkable |
| **Unsettled** | Out of sorts, restless or clingy |
| **Upset** | A difficult day |

**When it appears:** As soon as you click one of the five icons.
**What happens next:** The mood appears on the report the parent reads.
**Do you need to do anything?** **Yes — choose one deliberately.** A report left untouched
saves with a neutral, positive mood, so an unsettled day will not show as unsettled unless you
say so.
**Worth knowing:** **Great**, **Unsettled** and **Upset** are the three that read most clearly
back to a parent. If a child's day was genuinely difficult, pick **Unsettled** or **Upset**
*and* explain it in the **Note to parents** box — the written note is what carries the detail.

## Daily report — meals

Four buttons for each meal. The meals are **Breakfast**, **Snack AM**, **Lunch**, **Snack PM**
and **Milk**.

| Label | What it means | Colour when chosen |
|---|---|---|
| **All** | Ate everything offered | Green |
| **Most** | Ate the greater part | Blue |
| **Some** | Ate a little | Amber |
| **None** | Did not eat | Red |

The same applies to the afternoon snack: note it in the comment box, and the parent has the
full picture of the day.

## Daily report — health and temperature

**Health Status** offers **Healthy**, **Mild Concern** and **Sent Home**, and there is a
temperature box that flags a reading of 38°C or above on screen as you type it. These are
working aids while you complete the form, and **these two selections are not retained on the
saved report.**

So whenever a child is unwell, has been hurt, or has a raised temperature, follow this
procedure:

1. **Write it in the Note to parents box.** State what happened, when, what you did, and the
   actual temperature reading if one was taken. For example: *"Ayesha felt warm after lunch.
   Temperature taken at 13:40 was 38.2°C. She was kept cool and quiet, drank water, and we
   called you at 14:00."* Free text like this is saved, published and sent to the parent
   exactly as you wrote it.
2. **Tell the room lead in person, straight away.** No screen in Peekaboo alerts anybody — not
   the office, not the principal, not the parent. Nothing in the portal replaces speaking to a
   colleague about an unwell child.
3. **For anything ongoing** — an allergy, asthma, a dietary requirement — also record it on the
   child's **Health** section on the **Children** page, so that it travels with the child every
   day rather than sitting on a single report.

This is simply the correct way to record illness, injury and temperature. Write it down, and
say it out loud.

## Attainment levels

**Attainment levels are created by your nursery**, so the names on your screen are your own. The
portal ships with no built-in scale.

Each level carries a name, a mark that sets its order (lowest to highest), a colour, and a
description.

**Where you see them:** the **Attainment** page, and the rating dropdowns on **Observations**.
**When they appear:** In the dropdown against each objective you tag on an observation.
**What happens next:** The rating is stored with the observation and feeds the assessment
report.
**Do you need to do anything?** **Yes — every objective you tag must be given an attainment
level** before the observation will save. Setting the **Age Group Attainment** dropdown at the
top first gives each objective a default rating as you tick it, which saves a lot of clicking.
**For whoever sets them up:** create your levels **before** teachers start observing — without
them the rating dropdown is empty and no observation can be saved. Fill in the **Description**
for each level, since that is the text teachers choose from. And give each level a distinct
mark, because that number sets the order.

## Labels that look like statuses but are not

A few coloured pills look like states and are not. Worth recognising so you do not read meaning
into them.

**INV** — The sky-blue badge on every invoice card. A document label meaning "this is an
invoice", identical on every row. It says nothing about payment. *Where:* Invoices.

**The receipt badge** — The amber badge on a receipt card shows the **type** of receipt
document. There is no cleared or pending concept. Worth knowing: a cheque recorded on a receipt
counts as money received straight away, whatever its clearing date. Where no type has been set,
it reads **RV**. *Where:* Receipts.

**Service Type** — A catalogue classification on a service — **Services**, **Resources**,
**Inventory** or **Expense**. It describes what kind of item it is, not a state. *Where:*
Services.

**Tax Exempt** — A green **Yes** or red **No**, marking whether an item carries VAT. A setting,
not a status. On **Purchases**, set this toggle **before** entering lines, since changing it
afterwards refreshes the service list and clears the lines. *Where:* Services; Invoices;
Purchases.

**Balance colours** — On **Balance by Class**, **Billed** is always green with a `+`,
**Received** always red with a `-`, and **Balance** carries an `=`. **A family who owes you
money shows in grey; a family who has overpaid shows in red.** All figures are displayed
without a minus sign, so the colour and the prefix carry the direction. Read it deliberately —
**grey means owing, red means in credit.** *Where:* Balance by Class.

**Attachment badges** — On **Expenses**, small badges label the kind of file attached — Image,
PDF, Document, Sheet and so on — or **No file** where nothing has been attached. File labels,
not states. *Where:* Expenses.

**Trace Checks** — A **Yes** or **No** setting on a payment mode. A configuration option, not a
status. *Where:* Payment Modes.

**The Live pill** — The small green pill on the **Live Dashboard** header is decoration. It is
always shown and is not a connection indicator. *Where:* Live Dashboard.

**Popular** — The badge on one of the package cards is presentation rather than a
recommendation. *Where:* Packages.

**No statuses at all in finance.** Suppliers, Expenses, Expense Types, Payment Vouchers,
Purchases and Services carry no active/inactive setting and no approval state. And invoices
have no paid or unpaid marker — **Balance by Class** is how you see where a family stands.
