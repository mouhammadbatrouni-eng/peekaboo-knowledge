# Guide to Each Area of Peekaboo

This guide is a tour. It walks you through every area of the portal in the order the
sidebar shows it, and explains what each one is for, who normally works in it, what you
will see when you open it, and — most importantly — how it connects to everything else.

It is meant to be **read**, not looked up. If you want the numbered steps for a specific
task, use the How To guide instead. Read this one first, or read it in the evening of
your first week, and the step-by-step instructions will make far more sense.

Bold text is exactly what appears on your screen, so you can match the words on the page
to the words here.

A note before you start: **your sidebar may be shorter than the list below.** Peekaboo
only shows you the areas your role is allowed to open. If an area described here is not
in your menu, it simply is not part of your job, and that is normal.

---

## The one idea that explains most of the portal

Before the tour, here is the single idea that makes everything else click into place.

**Everything in Peekaboo points at the child record.** Attendance, the daily report that
goes home, observations of a child's learning, invoices, receipts — all of them attach
to one child on the **Children** page.

But a child is not placed in a room directly. The link between a child and a class is a
separate thing called a **Registration**, which you create inside the child's own record.
It ties the child to a class, a session time, a date range and the specific weekdays they
attend.

That one detail explains the most common puzzle new users hit: *"I added the child, but
the teacher can't see them."* Almost always, the child exists but has no registration. A
child with no registration is in the system, visible on the **Children** page, and
invisible everywhere else — no register, no daily report, no class list.

So the order of operations for a new starter is always: **create the child, then register
the child.** Two steps, not one.

## Your Dashboard

**Open this area:** [Live Dashboard](https://peek.peek-a-boo.app/app)

### What this area is for

**Live Dashboard** is the view from the office door. It is the page you land on when you
sign in, and it answers the three questions a manager asks at a glance: how full are we
today, how is the money looking this month, and how many families are enquiring about
places.

### Who normally uses it

The principal and the office team, mostly. Room staff tend to pass through it on their
way to the register. Anyone can be given access to it.

### What you will see

A header with today's date and a small green **Live** pill, then three tabs.

**Main Dashboard** shows three headline cards — **Children Present** (a figure like
`42 / 50` with an attendance percentage beneath), **Absent Today** (with the split
between absent and excused shown underneath), and **Active Staff**. Below them sits
**Classroom Occupancy**, one bar per room showing enrolled against capacity, then
**Late Arrivals** listing each child marked late today with their reason and arrival
time, and **Upcoming & Pinned**, which collects pinned announcements and the next few
calendar events.

**Finance** has a month selector and shows what was billed against what was collected
for that month, revenue broken down by age group, and outstanding balances.

**CRM** shows the admissions picture, with nine counter cards and a choice of three
views — **Calendar**, **Graphs** and **Details**.

### The key things you can do here

Read, and change the view. That is genuinely all. You can move the Finance month
backwards and forwards, switch the CRM between its three views, and export the CRM
figures to a spreadsheet. **Nothing on the dashboard can be edited.** Every number you
see was created by somebody on another page.

### How it connects to the rest of the portal

The dashboard is a mirror. It holds no data of its own — it reads what other pages have
recorded and draws it as figures and charts.

That means when a dashboard number looks wrong, **the dashboard is not where you fix
it.** Follow the number back to its source: the attendance figures come from
**Children Attendance**, the occupancy bars come from class capacities on **Classes** and
from registrations on **Children**, the Finance tab comes from **Invoices** and
**Receipts**, and the CRM tab comes from **Leads**.

### Worth knowing

**It refreshes itself about once a minute.** This is the only page in Peekaboo that does.
There is no refresh button and you do not need one — leave it open and the figures update
on their own. It also refreshes when you click back onto the browser tab after being away.

Everywhere else in the portal is the opposite: pages load once and stay still. If a
colleague changes something while you have a page open, reload the page to see it.

**Present and Absent will often not add up to your total roll**, and this is worth
understanding rather than worrying about. Children whose register has not been marked at
all count in neither figure. They are not "absent" — nobody has said anything about them
yet. If the numbers look short, the usual reason is an unmarked register, not a wrong
number. Marking every child every morning keeps the dashboard honest.

A child marked **Late** counts as present in the **Children Present** figure. That is
deliberate — they are in the building.

**Active Staff** counts who is employed and active on your staff list, not who has
clocked in today. For who is actually in the building right now, open **Staff
Attendance**.

**The enquiry cards use different wording from the Admissions page.** A stage that reads
one way on the dashboard may read differently on **Leads**. This matters most around the
tour stages, where the dashboard and the Leads page do not use the same words for the
same point in the journey.

The rule is simple and worth committing to memory: **treat the Leads page as the
authority on any enquiry's stage.** Use the dashboard for the general shape of the month
— are enquiries up, are we converting — and open **Leads** whenever you need to know where
one particular family actually stands.

Finally, the dashboard shows you your centre's position, not a per-room breakdown you can
filter. There is no campus filter and no date range beyond the Finance month selector.

## Your Nursery

This group holds your nursery's own settings. Two pages: **My Center** and **Packages**.
Principals also see a third, **Centers**, which is explained below because the similar
names cause real confusion.

### Centers vs My Center — read this once and it will never trouble you again

These two page names look almost identical and mean entirely different things.

**My Center** is *your nursery's own details.* Your name, your address, your phone
numbers, your email, your logo, and your session times. This is the page you want
whenever you think "our phone number has changed" or "we need to update the logo". Most
staff who have access to either page have this one.

**Centers** is a *multi-nursery screen that only the Principal can open.* It lists every
nursery on the platform, and it is where a brand-new nursery is set up along with its
first administrator login. It handles licence dates, capacity and banking references at
the platform level.

**The rule of thumb: anything about *our* nursery is My Center. Always.** If you do not
see **Centers** in your sidebar, nothing is missing — it is simply not part of your role,
and you will never need it.

**Open this area:** [My Center](https://peek.peek-a-boo.app/app/my-center) — your own
nursery's details.

**Open this area:** [Centers](https://peek.peek-a-boo.app/app/centers) — the multi-nursery
screen. Principals only. If your role does not include it, the link will show a short
**Access denied** panel, which is the account working as intended.

### My Center

**Open this area:** [My Center](https://peek.peek-a-boo.app/app/my-center)

**What this area is for.** Your nursery's own record — the details that appear on
documents and the session times that registrations are built from.

**Who normally uses it.** The principal or the office administrator. It is a page you
visit a few times a year, not daily.

**What you will see.** Your nursery's details laid out as a form — name, address, contact
numbers, email, logo — and a separate **Sessions** table listing your daily time bands,
each with a status of **Active** or **Not Active**.

**The key things you can do here.** Update your nursery's contact details, change the
logo, and add or edit the sessions your nursery offers.

**How it connects to the rest of the portal.** Sessions are the quiet but important part.
Every registration — every child placed into a class — must name a session. Those session
options come from here, and **only sessions marked Active appear in the dropdown.**

This produces a specific situation worth recognising: if somebody tells you the timing
dropdown is empty when they try to register a child, the answer is almost always here.
Either no session has been created yet, or the one they need is set to **Not Active**.
Switch it back on, or add the session, and registration proceeds normally.

**Worth knowing.** "Not Active" on this page means exactly the same as "Inactive"
elsewhere in the portal — the wording just differs slightly between screens. Set a session
inactive when you stop offering it; nothing is lost, and you can switch it back on at any
time.

### Packages

**Open this area:** [Packages](https://peek.peek-a-boo.app/app/packages)

**What this area is for.** Your nursery's own subscription to the Peekaboo software.

**Who normally uses it.** The principal or the owner. Almost nobody else needs it.

**What you will see.** A badge at the top of the page showing your current subscription
position, and a set of package cards you can buy from.

**The key things you can do here.** View your subscription and purchase or renew one.

**How it connects to the rest of the portal.** It does not connect to anything else. This
is the crucial point and the source of the only real confusion about this page.

**Packages has nothing whatsoever to do with billing parents.** It is the nursery paying
for the software, not families paying for childcare. If you are looking for a family's
fees, you want **Invoices**, **Receipts** and **Balance by Class** under Money In.

**Worth knowing.** While a subscription is live or a payment is still settling, the buy
button is unavailable — this is a deliberate safeguard against paying twice. One of the
package cards is marked **Popular**; treat that as decoration rather than a
recommendation. Subscription questions that cannot be resolved on this page are answered
by whoever supports your portal, not from inside Peekaboo.

## Daily Operations

This is the group room staff live in: the register, the report that goes home, and the
diary.

### Children Attendance vs Staff Attendance — the second pair worth separating

These are **two completely separate pages** with **different status options**, and mixing
them up is the second most common source of confusion after Centers and My Center.

**Children Attendance** marks the children. Its options are **Present**, **Absent**,
**Late** and **Excused**.

**Staff Attendance** clocks the employees in and out. Its options are **Present**,
**Absent**, **Late** and **On Leave**.

Notice the fourth option differs. **Excused is for children only** — an authorised absence
such as a holiday or a medical appointment. **On Leave is for staff only** — approved time
off. You cannot mark a child On Leave, and you cannot mark a staff member Excused. If you
find yourself hunting for a missing option, check which of the two pages you are actually
on.

Both pages work on **today only**. Neither has a date picker. For any other date, you use
**Attendance Reports**.

### Children Attendance

**Open this area:** [Children Attendance](https://peek.peek-a-boo.app/app/attendance)

**What this area is for.** The morning register, and the record of who is on the premises.
In a safeguarding sense it is the most important page in the building.

**Who normally uses it.** Room staff and teaching assistants, first thing every morning
and again at home time. The office keeps an eye on it.

**What you will see.** Today's date across the top, then four summary cards — **Present**,
**Absent**, **Late** and **Excused** — each showing a count.

Below them is a row of classroom filter buttons starting with **All**, and then the
register itself: one row per child, showing a coloured circle with their initials, their
name, their classroom, and a coloured status pill. If a child has an allergy recorded, the
word **Allergy** appears beneath their name in red.

Each row carries four quick buttons under **Update Status** — **Present**, **Late**,
**Absent** and **Excused** — plus **Edit** and, when appropriate, **Checkout**.

**The key things you can do here.** Mark each child's status with one click; check a child
out at home time; open **Edit** to set exact times or add a note; filter to a single room.

**How it connects to the rest of the portal.** The register is built from your **Children**
list, filtered to those with an active registration. Every active child appears every day,
whether or not anyone has marked them.

The link runs backwards too: if a child is missing from this page, they are not missing
from the nursery — they are missing a **registration**. Open the child on the **Children**
page and check the registration section.

The allergy warning under a child's name comes from the **Health** section of their record
on the **Children** page. It appears only for allergies recorded as proper health entries,
which is exactly why they should be recorded that way.

What you mark here flows onward to the **Live Dashboard** figures and to **Attendance
Reports**.

**Worth knowing.** A child who has not been marked shows **Not Marked** in grey. That is
not the same as absent — it means nobody has said anything yet. Clearing every **Not
Marked** before the morning is out is the single best habit in this module, because those
children are counted in neither the present nor the absent totals on the dashboard.

Marking a child **Present** or **Late** stamps their arrival time automatically. Marking
them **Absent** or **Excused** clears both times, which is correct — they were not here —
but do note that if you switch a status back afterwards you will need to re-enter the
times by hand in **Edit**.

There is no reason dropdown. Record the reason for an absence, and the reason for
lateness, in the **Notes** box inside **Edit**. Late arrivals with no note appear on the
dashboard as **No reason given**, so this is worth the extra few seconds.

**Mark the register as the morning happens, not at the end of the week.** Because this
page is today-only and past dates cannot be edited anywhere in the portal, a day that goes
unmarked stays unmarked. Build it into the arrival routine.

### Staff Attendance

**Open this area:** [Staff Attendance](https://peek.peek-a-boo.app/app/staff-attendance)

**What this area is for.** Clocking employees in and out, and recording who is absent or
on leave.

**Who normally uses it.** The principal, the office, or a room lead responsible for the
team.

**What you will see.** The same shape as the children's board — today's date, four summary
cards (**Present**, **Absent**, **Late**, **On Leave**), then one row per employee with a
status badge, times, and buttons.

**The key things you can do here.** **Check in** an employee, **Checkout** at the end of
the shift, mark someone **Absent** or **Leave**, and open **Edit** for exact times or to
mark someone **Late**.

**How it connects to the rest of the portal.** The rows come from your **Staff** list.
What you record here feeds **Attendance Reports**, which is where you look up any past
date.

It is worth knowing what this page does *not* connect to. It does not calculate hours
worked, and it does not accumulate lateness totals. It is a daily record, not a payroll
system.

**Worth knowing.** **Late** is the one status without a quick button — set it by opening
**Edit** and choosing it from the status dropdown. That is the expected route, so do not
go looking for a button that is not there.

One label varies slightly: the quick button reads **Leave** while the summary card and the
edit dropdown read **On Leave**. All three mean the same thing.

The quick buttons for **Absent** and **Leave** clear the times, while the edit dialog
leaves times as you have set them. If you are marking someone absent, the quick button
gives the cleanest record.

**Access to this page comes bundled with the children's register.** They share a single
permission, so anyone who can mark the children's register can also see the staff
clock-in sheet. Bear that in mind when deciding who gets access.

### Daily Reports

**Open this area:** [Daily Reports](https://peek.peek-a-boo.app/app/daily-reports)

**What this area is for.** The care record that goes home to each family — what the child
ate, whether they napped, how they seemed, and what you would like the parent to know.

**Who normally uses it.** Room staff and teaching assistants, building it up across the
day.

**What you will see.** The page has two views.

The **list view** opens with a date picker and a classroom dropdown, and a count line
reading something like `3 published · 5 drafts`. Down the left is one card per child,
each showing their name, their room and their report's state — the mood icon plus
**Published** in green or **Draft** in amber, or a red **Report not created** if nothing
has been written yet. There is an envelope button on each card for emailing. The right
panel shows the selected child's full report.

The **New Report** view is the data-entry workspace: pick a class, pick a child, and fill
in the sections — mood under **Today I Was**, **Meals & Drinks**, **Nap Time**,
**Daily Care**, **Items Needed**, **Health Status**, temperature, and the free-text boxes
including **Note to parents**.

**The key things you can do here.** Build a report across the day and **Save Draft** as
you go; **Publish** it when it is finished; **Publish & Email** to send it to the family;
email an already-saved report with the envelope button; and edit the note afterwards with
the pencil icon.

**How it connects to the rest of the portal.** Three connections matter.

First, **which children appear depends on their registration.** A child shows up on a
given date only if their registration is active, covers that date, and includes that
weekday. A child enrolled Monday and Wednesday will genuinely not appear on a Tuesday.
That is enrolment working correctly, not a fault — but it surprises people, so it is worth
knowing before you go hunting.

Second, **the report is emailed to the parent on the child's record.** A child with no
parent recorded on the **Children** page has nobody to email. If the family needs to
receive reports, make sure the parent details are filled in.

Third, **what appears on the report form is set per class.** Two rooms can have genuinely
different report forms — a baby room showing milk and nappies, a pre-school room not. Those
switches live on the **Classes** page under Early Childhood, not here.

**Worth knowing.** **Publish and email are two different actions.** Publishing marks the
report as finished in the portal. It does not send anything. To get the report to the
family, use **Publish & Email**, or press the envelope button on the card afterwards. This
is the single most useful thing to know about this page.

Unlike the attendance boards, **this page has a date picker**, so you can review and write
reports for other dates.

And now the most important procedure on this page:

> ### Recording illness, injury and temperature
>
> The **Health Status** buttons and the **temperature** box are working aids for you while
> you fill the form in. The temperature box will helpfully flag a reading of 38°C or above
> on screen as you type it. **These two selections are not retained on the saved report.**
>
> So whenever a child is unwell, has been hurt, or has a raised temperature, do both of
> these things:
>
> 1. **Write it in the Note to parents box.** Say what happened, when, what you did, and
>    the actual temperature reading if one was taken. For example: *"Ayesha felt warm
>    after lunch. Temperature taken at 13:40 was 38.2°C. She was kept cool and quiet, drank
>    water, and we called you at 14:00."* Free text like this is saved, published and sent
>    to the parent exactly as you wrote it.
> 2. **Tell the room lead in person, straight away.** No screen in Peekaboo alerts anybody
>    — not the office, not the principal, not the parent. Nothing in the portal is a
>    substitute for speaking to a colleague about an unwell child.
>
> For anything ongoing — an allergy, asthma, a dietary requirement — also record it on the
> child's **Health** section on the **Children** page, so that it follows the child every
> day rather than sitting on one report.
>
> This is simply the correct procedure. Write it down, say it out loud.

A few smaller points that help the reports read accurately. The nap **times** you enter
are what is kept, so fill those in whenever a child sleeps. In the meals section, record
what you can in the comment box beside each meal — a short note is always clearer than a
button alone. And because an untouched report saves with a neutral mood, take the moment
to pick the mood that actually matches the child's day.

The footer of a report always reads **Parent has not viewed yet**. That line is not a read
receipt and does not update. If you need to know a parent has seen something, ask them.

Reports cannot be printed from the portal. The envelope button emails them, which is the
route to the family.

### Calendar

**Open this area:** [Calendar](https://peek.peek-a-boo.app/app/calendar)

**What this area is for.** The nursery diary — holidays, trips, performances, meetings and
key dates.

**Who normally uses it.** The office and the principal keep it; everybody reads it.

**What you will see.** A month grid with weeks starting on Monday, today's date in a
filled circle, and coloured chips for each event. Days with more than three events show
**+N more**. Above it are a class filter strip starting with **All Classes**, arrows to
move between months, a **Today** button, and a toggle between **Month** and **Agenda**.
An **Event types:** legend across the bottom explains the colours.

**The key things you can do here.** Add, edit and delete events, filter to one class, move
between months, and switch between the month grid and the agenda list.

**How it connects to the rest of the portal.** Events can be tagged to a class, and those
class options come from **Classes**. Leave every class unselected and the event applies to
the whole nursery. Upcoming events also surface on the **Live Dashboard** in the
**Upcoming & Pinned** panel.

**Worth knowing.** The field labelled **Branches** in the event dialog actually lists your
**classes**, and it takes one at a time. Pick a single class, or leave them all unselected
for a nursery-wide event.

**There are no recurring events.** A weekly session needs to be entered for each
occurrence. A multi-day event is fine — set an end date and it spans the days as one
continuous event.

The event type controls the colour and nothing else. It sends no reminders and notifies
nobody. If parents need to know about an event, tell them through **Messages**.

Only two views exist, **Month** and **Agenda**. There is no week or day view.

## People

The two rosters everything else refers back to.

### Children

**Open this area:** [Children](https://peek.peek-a-boo.app/app/children)

**What this area is for.** The record of every child on your books. This is the biggest and
most important area of the portal — the child record is what attendance, reports,
observations, invoices and receipts all attach to.

**Who normally uses it.** The registrar and the office, day to day. Room staff often have
read access to check details.

**What you will see.** A grid of cards, one per child, each showing their photo or a
coloured circle of initials, their name, an **Active** or **Inactive** pill, their age,
today's attendance, gender, nationality, and the parent's name and phone. If the child has
allergies recorded, a red triangle with a count appears. Above the grid is a count line
reading `N enrolled`, and a filter row — a search box reading **Search by name or ID…**,
plus **All Status**, **All Classes** and **All Age Groups** dropdowns.

Clicking a card opens the child form, which is where the real work happens. It has four
tabs: **Child Data**, **Health**, **Pickup** and **Registrations**.

**The key things you can do here.** Add a child; edit their details; record allergies,
medical conditions and dietary needs under **Health**; list the adults authorised to
collect them under **Pickup**; and place them into a class under **Registrations**. You
can also search and filter, and set a child to Inactive when they leave.

**How it connects to the rest of the portal.** This page is the hub, so the connections
run in every direction.

**Registration is the connection that matters most.** Under the **Registrations** tab you
link the child to a class, a session, a date range and the weekdays they attend. Until
that exists, the child appears in no register, no daily report and no class list.

It also means **moving a child to a different room is an edit to their registration**, not
to the child. There is no class field on the child's main details to change. The same goes
for changing which days they attend or their session time — all registration edits.

The **Health** tab is what produces the red allergy warning on the attendance board and on
the child's card. An allergy typed only into a free-text notes box will not raise that
warning, which is precisely why allergies belong in a proper health entry. The **Dietary**
type is where special diets go — there is no separate dietary screen anywhere in the
portal.

The parent recorded here is who receives the child's daily reports and the messages you
publish.

Invoices and receipts are raised against **the child**, not the parent. So is the figure on
**Balance by Class**.

Class and age group options in the filters come from **Classes** and **Age Groups** under
Early Childhood.

**Worth knowing.** **Save the child first.** Health records, pickup people and
registrations can only be added once the child record itself has been saved and exists. Put
the name and birth date in, save, then go back in and complete the rest. The form guides
you this way, and knowing it in advance saves a puzzled minute.

**Child Name** and **Birth Date** are the only two fields that must be filled to save. The
photo shows a red asterisk but the record will save without one.

**When a child leaves, set their status to Inactive rather than deleting them.** This keeps
their history — attendance, reports, observations and finance records — intact and
readable. Deleting is permanent, and in any case the portal will refuse to delete a child
who has related records, showing a message that related records exist. Inactive is the
right tool and the one to reach for.

One small practical note on the filters: the **Clear** button resets the search, class and
age filters but leaves the **Status** filter where you set it. If your list looks unexpectedly
short or long, glance at the status dropdown and set it back to **All Status** by hand.

### Staff

**Open this area:** [Staff](https://peek.peek-a-boo.app/app/staff)

**What this area is for.** The employee register — who works here, how to reach them, and
what they can see in the portal.

**Who normally uses it.** The principal and the office administrator.

**What you will see.** Two views, toggled by icon buttons in the header. **Card view** is
the default and shows one card per employee with photo, name, an **Active** or **Inactive**
pill, role, email, phone and username. **Table view** shows the same people as rows with
columns for **Employee Name**, **Picture**, **Role**, **Username**, **Password**,
**Email** and **Phone**.

The staff form has two tabs: **Details**, which carries identity, contact, login and role,
and **More...**, which holds emergency contact, qualifications, employment dates and notes.

**The key things you can do here.** Add a staff member, edit their details, set or reset
their portal password, assign their role, and set them Active or Inactive.

**How it connects to the rest of the portal.** **Creating a staff member creates their
portal login.** The username, password and role are captured on the same form as their
employment details — there is no separate account-creation screen.

The **Role** you pick here decides what that person sees in their sidebar. The roles
themselves, and what each one may open, are configured under **Roles & Access**. In short:
Roles & Access defines what a role can do; this page decides which role a person holds.

Staff appear as options elsewhere in the portal — as teachers to assign to a class on the
**Classes** page, as the named observer on **Observations**, and as rows on **Staff
Attendance**.

**Assigning a teacher to a class does not happen here.** It happens on the **Classes**
page, where you pick the teachers for each room.

**Worth knowing.** **The table view displays staff passwords in readable text**, and a
password column is genuinely sensitive. Treat this screen as confidential: use card view
by default, and be mindful of who can see your monitor when the table is open.

When someone leaves, set them **Inactive** rather than deleting them. Their attendance
history and their past observations stay attached and meaningful. The thumbs-up button on
the list flips status immediately with no confirmation step, so click it deliberately.

In table view you will see **Copy**, **PDF**, **Print**, **CSV** and an entries selector
along the toolbar. These are not wired up on this page — use the information on screen
directly. The sort arrows on the column headers are likewise decorative here; use the
search and filter controls to narrow the list.

If a staff member reports that a page is missing from their menu, the answer is nearly
always their role. Check the role on this page, check what that role is allowed to open
under **Roles & Access**, and have them sign out and back in afterwards so their menu
rebuilds.

## Messages

**Open this area:** [Messages](https://peek.peek-a-boo.app/app/messages)

**What this area is for.** The nursery's noticeboard to families — announcements,
newsletters, photos and videos, published to the parents' timeline in the separate parent
app.

**Who normally uses it.** The office, the principal, and room staff sharing photos of the
day.

**What you will see.** The page is headed **Communication**. Across the top is a strip of
six type tabs — **Announcement**, **Notification**, **Event**, **Newsletter**, **Media**
and **Video Media** — each with a small count badge. Below is a filter bar with
**Keyword**, **From**, **To**, a types dropdown and an **Unpublished** toggle, then the
history of everything published, shown either as **Timeline View** cards or **List View**
rows. Each item carries a status dot: **green for published, amber for unpublished**.

**The key things you can do here.** Compose and publish a post to selected families,
attach files, photos or a video, pin a notification, schedule a post for later, search the
history, and edit or delete something already published.

**How it connects to the rest of the portal.** You choose recipients as **children**, and
each child's parent receives the post. Those children come from the **Children** page, and
the class groupings come from **Classes**.

Pinned announcements appear on the **Live Dashboard** in the **Upcoming & Pinned** panel.

You will also notice some **Daily report** items in the history. Those arrive here
automatically when daily reports are published; you cannot create one from this page, and
there is no tab for them.

**Worth knowing.** **Messages goes one way.** It is a noticeboard, not an inbox. There are
no replies, no threads and no conversation view. Parents who need to reach you will do so
the way they always have — by phone, by email, or at the door. When you write a post,
write it as a notice rather than as the opening of a conversation.

**Clicking a type tab does two things at once**: it filters the history to that type *and*
opens the compose dialog. If you only meant to filter, press Escape or **Cancel** — the
filter stays applied.

A few rules that will save you a re-do. A **Title** is required. **At least one child must
be ticked** — selecting a class alone is not enough, so pick the class first and then tick
the children. And **choosing a class clears any children you had already ticked**, which is
why class first, children second is the order to work in.

On attachments: videos may be up to 35 MB and images up to 1 MB each. If one file in a
selection is too large, the whole selection is cleared rather than just that file, so add a
large file on its own to see clearly what happened.

**There are no read receipts.** The portal cannot tell you whether a parent opened a post.
The only status shown is your own publishing state — whether *you* released it.

You can edit a post after publishing, and you can delete one, but there is no recall. Once
something has gone out, assume it has been seen.

## Early Childhood

This group is the curriculum and assessment side of the portal: how learning is structured,
how teachers record what they see, and how that becomes a report for parents.

### How the pieces fit together

Worth reading before the individual pages, because the structure explains the order you
set things up in.

A **Curriculum** is your framework. It owns two things directly: the **Age Groups** you
band children into, and the **Attainment** levels that form your grading scale.

Within a curriculum, learning is divided into **Learn Areas** — the broad areas of
learning. Each area contains **Aspects**, the narrower strands inside it. Within those sit
the **Objectives**, the individual goals a child is assessed against, reached by combining
an aspect with an age group.

So the shape is: **Curriculum → Learn Area → Aspect → Objective**, with **Age Groups** and
**Attainment** hanging off the curriculum alongside.

Separately, a **Class** is a real room. It belongs to an age group and has teachers
assigned.

The daily work runs through this structure. A teacher records an **Observation** — I
watched this child, on this date, doing this — and tags it with the objectives it
demonstrated, each rated at an attainment level. Over a term those observations accumulate,
and **Assessment Report** turns them into the printable summary parents receive.

The practical consequence is worth stating plainly: **the assessment report reads
observations.** It does not hold judgements of its own. No observations in the date range
means no content in the report. Regular observing through the term is what makes reports
possible at the end of it.

### Classes

**Open this area:** [Classes](https://peek.peek-a-boo.app/app/early-childhood/classes)

**What this area is for.** Your actual rooms — name, capacity, which age group they belong
to, which teachers staff them, and what appears on that room's daily report.

**Who normally uses it.** The principal or the office administrator, at setup and when
staffing changes.

**What you will see.** **Card view** by default, one card per class with its logo or a
coloured square, the class name, an **Active** or **Inactive** pill, the description, the
centre name and the age group. **Table view** shows more: **Status**, **Class Name**,
**Center**, **Class Logo**, **Age Group**, **Class Description**, **No. of Children**,
**Teacher Ratio**, **Teachers** shown as avatar chips, and actions.

**The key things you can do here.** Create and edit classes, set capacity, assign teachers,
and set which rows appear on that class's daily report.

**How it connects to the rest of the portal.** Classes sit at the centre of the daily
routine. **Every class must belong to an age group**, so age groups — and therefore a
curriculum — need to exist first.

Classes then feed almost everything: the classroom filters on **Children Attendance**, the
room dropdown on **Daily Reports**, the class picker when registering a child on
**Children**, the class selection on **Observations** and **Assessment Report**, the class
chips on **Balance by Class**, and the class tagging on **Calendar** and **Messages**.

**This is where a teacher is assigned to a room** — not on the **Staff** page.

And this is where the **daily report settings** live. The switches on a class record decide
which sections teachers see when filling in that room's reports, which is how a baby room
and a pre-school room end up with sensibly different forms.

**Worth knowing.** Capacity feeds the occupancy bars and the over-capacity warning on the
**Live Dashboard**. Keeping it accurate makes that dashboard panel genuinely useful.

The search box and status filter appear in **table view** only. Card view shows every
class, so switch to table view when you want to find one quickly in a longer list.

When a room closes, set it **Inactive** rather than deleting it. Deleting a class leaves
registrations, attendance history and observations pointing at a room that no longer
exists, and children in it effectively lose their placement. Inactive keeps the history
intact.

### Curriculum

**Open this area:** [Curriculum](https://peek.peek-a-boo.app/app/early-childhood/curriculum)

**What this area is for.** The framework your nursery assesses against, and the top of the
whole early-years structure.

**Who normally uses it.** The principal or a curriculum lead, at setup. It is rarely
touched afterwards.

**What you will see.** A split screen — the list of curricula on the left, the add and edit
form on the right. Each entry shows its name and status.

**The key things you can do here.** Create, edit and deactivate a curriculum.

**How it connects to the rest of the portal.** Everything else in this group depends on it.
A curriculum owns its **Age Groups** and its **Attainment** levels, and it contains the
**Learn Areas** that hold aspects and objectives. **Set this up first** — nothing else in
Early Childhood can be built until a curriculum exists.

**Worth knowing.** Deactivating a curriculum keeps it and everything beneath it intact
while taking it out of everyday use. The status word on this page always displays in a
green badge; read the word itself — **Active** or **Inactive** — rather than the colour.

### Age Groups

**Open this area:** [Age Groups](https://peek.peek-a-boo.app/app/early-childhood/age-groups)

**What this area is for.** The age bands your nursery uses — Playgroup, Nursery, KG1, or
whatever your setting calls them — each defined by an age range in months.

**Who normally uses it.** The principal or curriculum lead, at setup.

**What you will see.** A split screen listing your age groups with their codes,
descriptions and the age range each covers.

**The key things you can do here.** Create, edit and remove age groups.

**How it connects to the rest of the portal.** Age groups belong to a curriculum, so a
curriculum must exist first.

They then do three jobs. **Every class must have one**, so age groups must exist before you
can create a class. They appear as a filter on the **Children** page, matching each child's
age in months against the band. And they combine with an aspect to reach the **objectives**
a teacher can tag on an observation.

They are also recorded on admissions enquiries, as the band the family is asking about.

**Worth knowing.** Set the month ranges carefully, because the **Children** page filter
uses them to work out which children fall into which band. Gaps or overlaps in the ranges
show up there first.

### Attainment

**Open this area:** [Attainment](https://peek.peek-a-boo.app/app/early-childhood/attainment)

**What this area is for.** Your grading scale — the levels a teacher picks from when rating
how securely a child has grasped an objective.

**Who normally uses it.** The principal or curriculum lead, at setup.

**What you will see.** A table of levels, each with a name, a numeric mark, a colour and a
description.

**The key things you can do here.** Create, edit and remove attainment levels.

**How it connects to the rest of the portal.** Attainment levels belong to a curriculum.
They appear in **Observations**, where every objective a teacher tags **must** carry an
attainment level before the observation can be saved. The colour you choose tints that
rating on screen.

**Worth knowing.** **There is no built-in scale.** Peekaboo does not ship with
emerging/developing/secure or anything else — every level is one your nursery creates. If
anyone asks what the levels mean, the answer comes from your setting, not from the
software.

**Set these up before teachers start observing.** Without attainment levels the rating
dropdown has nothing in it, and observations cannot be saved at all. It is a five-minute
job that unblocks the whole module.

Two practical tips. **Fill in the Description field**, because that is the text teachers
actually choose from in the observation dropdowns — if the description is blank or differs
from the name, the same level reads differently in the two places. And give each level a
distinct **mark**, since that number drives the order they appear in, lowest to highest.

### Learn Area

**Open this area:** [Learn Area](https://peek.peek-a-boo.app/app/early-childhood/learning-area)

**What this area is for.** The broad areas of learning within your curriculum, and the
aspects inside each one.

**Who normally uses it.** The principal or curriculum lead, at setup.

**What you will see.** A list of learning areas, each with its name, colour and status.
Opening an area reveals the **aspects** within it.

**The key things you can do here.** Create and edit learning areas, and add aspects inside
them.

**How it connects to the rest of the portal.** Learn areas belong to a curriculum. Aspects
live inside an area, and an aspect combined with an age group is what produces the
**objectives** a teacher tags on an observation. **Objectives only become visible once both
an aspect and an age group are chosen** on the observation screen, which is the practical
reason aspects matter.

A learning area is also the required subject of an **Assessment Report** — each report
covers one child in one learning area.

**Worth knowing.** You may see a learning area referred to as a *main domain* and an aspect
as a *sub-domain* on the printed assessment report. Same things, different words for the
parent-facing document.

The status pill on this page always displays green; read the word rather than the colour.

### Assessments

**Open this area:** [Assessments](https://peek.peek-a-boo.app/app/early-childhood/assessment)

**What this area is for.** A small landing page with a few summary counters and links into
the two pages where the work actually happens.

**Who normally uses it.** Teachers and the principal, passing through.

**What you will see.** Three counter cards — **Total Assessments**, **Children Assessed**
and **Total Comments** — and two cards headed **Observation Workflow** and **Assessment
Reporting**, with **Open Observations** and **Open Assessment Report** buttons.

**The key things you can do here.** Read the counters and click through. Nothing can be
created or edited on this page.

**How it connects to the rest of the portal.** It sits above **Observations** and
**Assessment Report**, which are indented beneath it in the sidebar. Its figures come from
generated assessment reports.

**Worth knowing.** **Total Assessments counts generated reports, not observations.** A
nursery with plenty of recorded observations but no generated reports yet will correctly
see zero here. To see observations, open **Observations**, where they are listed in full.

### Observations

**Open this area:** [Observations](https://peek.peek-a-boo.app/app/early-childhood/observation)

**What this area is for.** The heart of the curriculum module — a teacher recording what
they saw a child do, and linking it to the objectives it demonstrates.

**Who normally uses it.** Teachers and teaching assistants, through the week.

**What you will see.** The observation form at the top, as a four-column grid: **Class**,
**Teacher**, **Children**, **Date**, then **Observation Title**, **Notes**, **Observation**
and **Next Step**.

Beneath it sits a two-panel row. On the left is the objective selector, with four stacked
rows of filter pills — **Programs**, **Learn Areas**, **Learn Aspects** and **Age Groups**
— an **Age Group Attainment** dropdown, and the objectives table. On the right is the
selected-objectives panel, headed **View Selected Objectives for {child}** with a
**N selected** counter.

At the bottom are **Save Observation** and **Clear**, and below those the table of
observations already saved.

**The key things you can do here.** Record an observation, tag it with as many objectives
as apply, rate each objective at an attainment level, and save. You can also view, edit and
delete observations you have already saved.

**How it connects to the rest of the portal.** Observations pull from almost everything in
this group. The **Class** list comes from **Classes**, the teachers come from **Staff**,
the children come from the chosen class's registrations, the objectives come from the
**Curriculum → Learn Area → Aspect** structure combined with **Age Groups**, and the
ratings come from **Attainment**.

Forward, observations feed **Assessment Report**. This is the connection that matters most
to teachers: **assessment reports are built from observations recorded in the date range.**
Observe regularly through the term and the reports write themselves; leave it to the last
week and there will be nothing to report on.

**Worth knowing.** **Objectives appear only once you have chosen both an aspect and an age
group.** Until then the table reads **No data available in table**. That is the filter
cascade working as designed, not an empty curriculum — work down the pill rows in order.

**Every objective you tag must be given an attainment level** before the observation will
save. Setting the **Age Group Attainment** dropdown first gives each objective a default
rating as you tick it, which saves a lot of clicking.

Put your written detail in the **Observation** and **Next Step** boxes. Those are the two
that carry the record forward, and **Next Step** is especially valuable when the report is
written later. Keep them as full as you can — they are what makes an observation useful
three months on.

There is no draft state here and no publish step. **Save Observation** records it
immediately. Saved observations stay fully editable afterwards, with one sensible
exception: **Class**, **Teacher** and **Child** are fixed once saved, and show
**(Cannot be changed)**. Everything else, including the objectives and their ratings, can
be revised. If you picked the wrong child, delete the observation and record it again.

Changing the class clears the selected child and any chosen objectives, and changing the
child clears the objectives. Settle on the class and child first, then build the objective
list.

### Assessment Report

**Open this area:** [Assessment Report](https://peek.peek-a-boo.app/app/early-childhood/assessment-report)

**What this area is for.** The per-child, per-learning-area summary of progress that
parents receive at the end of a term.

**Who normally uses it.** Teachers and the principal, at reporting time.

**What you will see.** Three stacked sections.

At the top, the generate filters: **From Date**, **To Date**, **Class**, and a **Generate
Report** button.

In the middle, once you have generated, the **Generated Reports** table — one row per child
with columns **Child Name**, **Area**, **Comments** and **Actions**. The **Area** column is
a dropdown, **Comments** holds one or more comment boxes with a **+ Add Comment** button,
and **Actions** carries **Save Comments**.

At the bottom, the saved reports table, with columns including **Children**, **Class
Name**, **Assessment Reports**, **Area**, **From/To** and **Comments**. The
**Assessment Report** button opens the printable view, and **Show Comment** and **Update**
let you review and revise what was written.

**The key things you can do here.** Generate the reports for a class over a date range,
choose a learning area for each child, write comments, save them, and open the printable
version to print or save as a PDF through your browser.

**How it connects to the rest of the portal.** This page reads **Observations** — so the
date range you choose should cover the term you observed in. The **Class** list comes from
**Classes** and the **Area** options from **Learn Area**.

**Worth knowing.** The **Class** dropdown shows **All classes** by default, but generating
requires you to pick **one specific class**. Change that dropdown every time before
pressing **Generate Report**.

**Reports are saved one child at a time.** Each row needs its area chosen and at least one
comment written, then **Save Comments** for that row. A saved row disappears from the
generated table and moves down into the saved reports table — that is the row being filed,
not lost. For a class of twenty, expect twenty saves, so set aside a proper block of time.

If a report comes out thin, the reason is nearly always the date range: **no observations in
the window means no content.** Widen the dates, or check the observations were recorded
against the right class.

To produce a PDF, open the printable view and use your browser's print dialog, choosing
"Save as PDF". That is the standard route for printable documents throughout the portal.

## Money Out

Five pages covering what the nursery spends. They share a supplier list but are otherwise
independent records.

### An important point about how these five relate

It is natural to assume these pages form a chain — that a purchase becomes an expense,
which becomes a payment voucher. **They do not.** They are **three independent records that
happen to share a supplier**, each answering a different question:

- A **Purchase** records *what we ordered*, itemised line by line.
- An **Expense** records *a cost we incurred*, with the receipt or invoice scanned and
  attached.
- A **Payment Voucher** records *money actually leaving the bank*.

Recording one does not create or update another. For a single transaction you may want all
three, or just one — that is your nursery's bookkeeping decision.

The practical consequence: **keeping them consistent is a human job.** Agree a convention
with your accountant — for instance, always raise the expense with its receipt attached and
only raise a purchase for itemised orders — and write it down. The software will not stop
you recording the same spend twice, so an agreed habit is your safeguard.

### Suppliers

**Open this area:** [Suppliers](https://peek.peek-a-boo.app/app/suppliers)

**What this area is for.** The address book of everyone you buy from or pay out to.

**Who normally uses it.** The accountant or office administrator.

**What you will see.** A split screen — the supplier list on the left with columns
**Supplier Name**, **Phone**, **Email** and **VAT**, and the add/edit form on the right. A
search box reads **Search suppliers...**.

**The key things you can do here.** Add, edit and remove suppliers.

**How it connects to the rest of the portal.** A supplier is required on every **Expense**,
every **Purchase** and every **Payment Voucher**. This page has to be populated before any
of those three can be recorded.

**Worth knowing.** Only **Supplier Name** and a valid **Supplier VAT** between 0 and 100
are needed to save; everything else is optional. The VAT figure here is the supplier's own
rate for reference — it is not carried automatically into expense or purchase lines, which
take their VAT from the service or from what you type.

Suppliers have no active/inactive setting. When one stops trading, the tidiest approach is
to leave the record in place so historic purchases and expenses keep their counterparty,
rather than deleting it.

### Expenses

**Open this area:** [Expenses](https://peek.peek-a-boo.app/app/expenses)

**What this area is for.** Recording a cost the nursery has incurred, with proof attached.
This is the page for "we spent 350 on cleaning supplies from ABC Trading on the 14th, here
is the receipt".

**Who normally uses it.** The accountant or office administrator.

**What you will see.** A table with columns including **Date**, **Supply** (which shows the
expense type), **Supplier**, the amount, and an **Attachment** column showing a small badge
for the file type, or **No file** where nothing is attached.

**The key things you can do here.** Record an expense, categorise it, attach the receipt
scan, and edit or delete entries.

**How it connects to the rest of the portal.** Every expense needs a **Supplier** and an
**Expense Type**, so both of those lists must exist first. Expenses stand alone otherwise —
they do not flow into a voucher or come from a purchase.

**Worth knowing.** This is the one page in Money Out built around **proof of spend**.
Attach the receipt or invoice scan as a matter of routine; it is what makes the record
useful at audit.

The column headed **Supply** shows the expense type. Treat "Supply" and "Expense Type" as
the same thing on this page.

There is no approval step. An expense is recorded as soon as it is saved, so agree
internally who is allowed to enter them.

### Expense Types

**Open this area:** [Expense Types](https://peek.peek-a-boo.app/app/expense-types)

**What this area is for.** Your spending categories — Cleaning, Food & Catering,
Stationery, Utilities, Maintenance.

**Who normally uses it.** The accountant, at setup and occasionally after.

**What you will see.** A split screen with **Type Name** and **Description** columns on the
left and a two-field form on the right.

**The key things you can do here.** Add, edit and remove categories.

**How it connects to the rest of the portal.** These are the options in the category
dropdown on the **Expenses** page, and **at least one must exist before an expense can be
saved.**

**Worth knowing.** Only **Type Name** is required. Set up a sensible handful at the start
with your accountant — categories that match how you actually report — because changing
them later means revisiting past expenses.

### Payment Voucher

**Open this area:** [Payment Voucher](https://peek.peek-a-boo.app/app/payment-voucher)

**What this area is for.** Recording money actually leaving the nursery to a supplier.
Where an expense says "we incurred this cost", a voucher says "we paid this out, on this
date, by this method".

**Who normally uses it.** The accountant.

**What you will see.** A table with **Date**, **Type**, **Number**, **Supplier** and
**Amount** columns. The form carries a header block and payment lines, so one voucher can
hold several payments.

**The key things you can do here.** Record a payment out, split it across several lines,
and edit or delete vouchers.

**How it connects to the rest of the portal.** A voucher needs a **Supplier** and each
payment line needs a **Payment Mode**, which comes from the Money In group. Both lists must
exist first.

**Worth knowing.** Several payment lines on one voucher is the feature to reach for when
settling a supplier in instalments, or splitting a payment across a cheque and a transfer.

There is no approval workflow. A voucher is a record of payment, not a request for one, so
the approval itself happens by your own process outside the portal.

### Purchases

**Open this area:** [Purchases](https://peek.peek-a-boo.app/app/purchases)

**What this area is for.** Recording what was bought from a supplier, itemised line by line
with quantities, fees and VAT.

**Who normally uses it.** The accountant or office administrator.

**What you will see.** A table with **Date**, **Type**, **Purchase#**, **Supplier** and
**Amount** columns, the amount being VAT-inclusive. The form has a header block and then
the purchase lines, with an **Add Purchase** button inside the form that adds a further
line.

**The key things you can do here.** Record an itemised purchase, add and remove lines, and
edit or delete purchases.

**How it connects to the rest of the portal.** A purchase needs a **Supplier**, and each
line names a **Service** from the **Services** catalogue — the same catalogue used for
invoicing. Picking a service pre-fills the fee and VAT on the line, which you can then
adjust.

**Worth knowing.** One thing to do in the right order: **set the Tax Exempt toggle before
you enter any lines.** Switching it afterwards refreshes the service list and clears the
lines you have entered. Set it first and the form behaves predictably.

Note that the page has two buttons reading very similarly — the one at the top right opens
a new purchase, while the one inside the form adds a line to the purchase you are working
on.

## Money In

Five pages covering the money families pay you.

### How the money-in pages relate

**Services → Invoices → Receipts → Balance by Class.**

A **Service** is a thing you charge for, with a default fee and VAT. An **Invoice** bills a
named child for one or more services. A **Receipt** records money received from that
child's family. **Balance by Class** shows the difference.

Two things are worth understanding clearly, because they shape how the whole group is used.

**Invoices and receipts are both raised against the child**, not the parent. The child
record is the account the money hangs off. So when you are looking for a family's finances,
you look under the child's name.

**A receipt is not attached to a particular invoice.** You record that a family paid; you
do not tick off which bill it settles. There is no paid or unpaid marker on an invoice, and
no way to mark one paid — that is simply not how this portal models it.

Instead, **Balance by Class** answers the money question by comparing totals: everything
billed for a child against everything received from them. That page is your
accounts-receivable view, and the reason the others feed into it.

### Services

**Open this area:** [Services](https://peek.peek-a-boo.app/app/services)

**What this area is for.** The catalogue of everything you charge for, and the price list
behind it — tuition, transport, uniform, lunch plan.

**Who normally uses it.** The accountant or principal, at setup and at each price review.

**What you will see.** A table with **Service Name**, **Service Fee**, **Service Type**,
**Service VAT**, **Service Cost** and **Tax Exempt** columns. **Service Type** shows as a
coloured pill and **Tax Exempt** as a green **Yes** or red **No**.

**The key things you can do here.** Add, edit and remove services and their default fees
and VAT rates.

**How it connects to the rest of the portal.** **You cannot raise an invoice without at
least one service**, so this is the first page to set up in the group. When a service is
picked on an invoice line, its fee and VAT pre-fill automatically. The same catalogue is
reused by **Purchases** on the spending side.

**Worth knowing.** Reassuring and important: **changing a service's fee does not change
invoices you have already raised.** Each invoice line stored its own fee and VAT at the
time it was saved. New invoices pick up the new price; historic ones keep what was actually
charged. You can run a price increase without disturbing your records.

**Service Cost** is what the item costs the nursery. It is recorded for your own margin
analysis and never appears on what you bill a family.

### Invoices

**Open this area:** [Invoices](https://peek.peek-a-boo.app/app/invoices)

**What this area is for.** Billing a named child's family for services.

**Who normally uses it.** The accountant.

**What you will see.** Two panels. On the left, a scrollable list of invoice cards, each
showing the date, the child's name, a sky-blue **INV** badge and the amount. On the right,
a full printable preview of the selected invoice with the nursery header, the **To:**
block, the invoice number, a line table with **Service**, **QTY**, **Fee**, **Disc%**,
**Disc Amnt**, **VAT%**, **VAT Amnt** and **Total** columns, then **Total VAT** and
**TOTAL**.

**The key things you can do here.** Raise an invoice against a child, add service lines
with quantities and discounts, preview it, print it, email it, and edit or delete it.

**How it connects to the rest of the portal.** An invoice names a **Child** from the
**Children** page and at least one **Service**. Every invoice raised increases the **Billed**
figure for that child on **Balance by Class**, and feeds the **Finance** tab of the
**Live Dashboard**.

**Worth knowing.** The **INV** badge on every card is a document label meaning "this is an
invoice". It is not a status and carries no payment information — every invoice shows it.

**There is no paid or unpaid marker**, and no way to mark an invoice paid. To see where a
family stands, use **Balance by Class**.

Because there are no statuses, any invoice can be edited or deleted at any time regardless
of age. That is worth an internal rule: **deleting an invoice reduces what a child appears
to have been billed**, which changes their balance. Correct mistakes by editing where you
can, and agree who is permitted to delete.

### Receipts

**Open this area:** [Receipts](https://peek.peek-a-boo.app/app/receipts)

**What this area is for.** Recording money received from a family.

**Who normally uses it.** The accountant, and reception when taking a payment.

**What you will see.** The same two-panel shape as Invoices. Each list card shows the date,
the child's name, an amber badge with the receipt type, and the amount. The right panel is
a printable receipt.

**The key things you can do here.** Record a payment, split it across several payment
lines, print a receipt for the family, and edit or delete it.

**How it connects to the rest of the portal.** A receipt names a **Child**, one **Service**,
and one or more payment lines each naming a **Payment Mode**. It increases the **Received**
figure for that child on **Balance by Class**.

**Worth knowing.** Several payment lines on one receipt is how you handle a family paying
partly in cash and partly by card — one receipt, two lines, rather than two receipts.

The amber badge shows the **type** of receipt document. It is not a status; there is no
cleared or pending concept here. Worth knowing for reconciliation: **a cheque recorded here
counts as money received straight away**, regardless of when it clears.

As with invoices, deleting a receipt changes a family's apparent balance — it will look as
though they owe money again. Edit rather than delete wherever possible.

### Payment Modes

**Open this area:** [Payment Modes](https://peek.peek-a-boo.app/app/payment-modes)

**What this area is for.** The list of ways money moves — Cash, Cheque, Bank Transfer,
Credit Card — each mapped to an accounting code.

**Who normally uses it.** The accountant, at setup.

**What you will see.** A split screen with **Acc#**, **Desc** and **Chart ID** columns and
a form on the right.

**The key things you can do here.** Add, edit and remove payment modes.

**How it connects to the rest of the portal.** This is the one setup page shared by both
directions of money. Payment modes fill the payment dropdown on **Receipts** (money in) and
on **Payment Voucher** (money out), and **at least one must exist before either can be
saved.**

The **Desc** text is what everyone sees in those dropdowns, so write it the way you want it
read — "Bank Transfer" rather than an abbreviation.

**Worth knowing.** The accounting reference fields carry codes that post into your ledger.
**Get these from your accountant** rather than guessing — they connect Peekaboo to your
bookkeeping and the correct values depend on your own chart of accounts.

This page has no search box, which is fine because the list is naturally short.

### Balance by Class

**Open this area:** [Balance by Class](https://peek.peek-a-boo.app/app/balance-by-class)

**What this area is for.** The outstanding-fees report — the one place in Peekaboo that
answers "who owes us money?".

**Who normally uses it.** The accountant and the principal.

**What you will see.** Four columns. **Student** shows the student code and class on one
side, the child's name and parent's name on the other. Then **Billed** in green with a `+`,
**Received** in red with a `-`, and **Balance** with an `=`. Above the list sit a
**Search student...** box, an **As of** date picker, and class filter chips starting with
**All Classes**.

**The key things you can do here.** Look, search, filter by class, and set an **As of**
cut-off date. The page is entirely read-only.

**How it connects to the rest of the portal.** It reads **Invoices** and **Receipts** and
shows the difference per child, with classes from **Classes** supplying the filter chips.
It is derived entirely from those two pages, which is why **nothing here can be edited.** To
change a balance, amend the underlying invoice or receipt.

**Worth knowing.** Two points about reading this page, both worth pausing on because the
presentation is easy to misread.

**Every figure is shown without a minus sign.** The direction is carried by the `+`, `-`
and `=` prefixes and by the colour, not by a negative number.

**A family who owes you money shows a positive balance in grey. A family who has overpaid
shows in red.** That is the opposite of most people's instinct, so read the colour
deliberately: **grey with a figure means owing; red means in credit.** Since balance is
billed minus received, it follows logically — but it still catches people, so it is worth
saying out loud to anyone new to the page.

Despite the page name, **the figures are per child, not per class.** Class is a grouping
label and a filter. There are no class subtotals and no grand total, so a class figure means
adding the rows yourself.

The **As of** date sets an end point only; there is no start date, so the page always shows
each child's lifetime position up to that date. Leave it empty for the position today.

Because a receipt is not linked to an invoice, this page tells you a child's overall
position but not **which** invoice is outstanding. For that, open **Invoices** and compare.

Children with no invoices and no receipts still appear, showing zeros. That is intentional —
the list is built from your children, not from transactions.

## Reports & Admin

### Attendance Reports

**Open this area:** [Attendance Reports](https://peek.peek-a-boo.app/app/attendance-reports)

**What this area is for.** Attendance history — **the place to look up any past date**, for
children or for staff.

**Who normally uses it.** The principal and the office, for reviews, absence follow-ups and
inspections.

**What you will see.** A date range selector and a choice between children and staff, then
a grid with one row per person and one column per day. Each cell holds a small circle with
a single letter: **P** for present, **A** for absent, **L** for late, **V** for leave, **E**
for excused, and a hyphen where nothing was marked. A summary strip above gives totals —
present days, absent days, late days, not-marked days, and percentages. A legend explains
the letters.

**The key things you can do here.** Choose a date range, switch between children and staff,
read the grid and the summary, and export to CSV or print.

**How it connects to the rest of the portal.** It reads what was recorded on **Children
Attendance** and **Staff Attendance**. It holds no data of its own and adds nothing.

**Worth knowing.** **This page is read-only.** Cells cannot be clicked or corrected. Only
today's record can be changed, and only on the relevant attendance board. That is exactly
why marking the register on the day matters so much: **today's register is your only
opportunity to get that day right.**

Two letters worth knowing, because the on-screen legend does not spell out the second one:
**V** means on leave and appears on staff rows, while **E** means excused and appears on
children's rows. They look similar on screen — if you are looking at a child, an authorised
absence is **E**.

Weekends appear as columns but are left out of the totals and percentages, which is
correct. Public holidays are counted as ordinary working days, so bear that in mind when
reading an absence percentage over a holiday period.

The **Total Absent Days** figure combines absent, excused and leave. An authorised absence
counts the same as an unexplained one in that single total, so read the individual letters
in the grid when the distinction matters.

In the CSV and print exports each cell shows the full word rather than the letter, which
makes exported files easier to share with people who do not know the legend.

### Roles & Access

**Open this area:** [Roles & Access](https://peek.peek-a-boo.app/app/roles)

**What this area is for.** Deciding which parts of the portal each job role can open.

**Who normally uses it.** The system administrator. This page is restricted, and most staff
will not see it in their sidebar.

**What you will see.** A **Total roles** counter and an **Add role** button, then a list of
role cards each showing the role name, **Active** or **Inactive**, and a **Manage access**
button. Selecting a role opens the access panel on the right, with a counter reading
`N of M modules selected`, **Select all** and **Clear all** buttons, the module list grouped
into sections, and **Save access**.

**The key things you can do here.** Create roles, switch them active or inactive, and tick
which areas each role may open.

**How it connects to the rest of the portal.** This page decides what everybody else sees.
Each staff member is given a role on the **Staff** page, and the ticks here decide what that
role's sidebar contains and which pages it can open.

The names on the tiles are the portal's own internal names for its areas, and a few do not
match the sidebar wording. Two to recognise: **Our Loved Ones** unlocks the **Children**
page, and **Our Heroes** unlocks the **Staff** page. There is also a single tile covering
the whole attendance and daily-reports group — ticking it grants all four of **Children
Attendance**, **Staff Attendance**, **Attendance Reports** and **Daily Reports** together,
which is why those four always travel as a set.

**Worth knowing.** **Changes take effect when the user next signs in.** After adjusting a
role, ask the affected staff to sign out and back in so their menu rebuilds. This is the
answer to most "I still can't see it" messages.

Deactivating a role is gentler than deleting one, since staff carrying that role keep their
record intact.

Work in small steps: change one role, have someone check it, then move on. It is much easier
to see what a change did when it is the only change.

## Admissions

Peekaboo's admissions area is labelled CRM in places. **Here, CRM means parents enquiring
about a nursery place** — from the first phone call, through a tour, to enrolment. It is not
a sales system: there are no deals, no quotes and no revenue targets. When a colleague says
"check the CRM", they mean the enquiry list.

### Leads

**Open this area:** [CRM Leads](https://peek.peek-a-boo.app/app/crm/leads)

**What this area is for.** The working list of every enquiry the nursery has received, and
the place you follow each family up.

**Who normally uses it.** The registrar and the front desk, with the principal watching the
pipeline.

**What you will see.** The page is headed **CRM · Leads** with the subtitle **Overview of
Lead Management**.

The main list shows one row per enquiry: delete and edit circles, the **Student Name** with
the grade beneath it, **Phone**, **Email**, and a coloured **status badge** showing where
the family is in the journey. The badge is clickable — it opens the menu for moving the
enquiry along. Beside the list is a **Tasks** panel headed **Today**, showing upcoming
follow-ups.

Clicking a family's name opens the detail view in three columns. On the left, the family's
full details — **Student Name**, **Parent Name**, **Phone**, **Email**, **Grade**, and a
second block with **Term**, **DOB**, **Age**, **Nationality**, **Operator**, **Expected
Joining**, **Source**, **Referral**, **Previous School**, **Inquiry Date** and more. In the
middle, **Tasks** with a **+** button. On the right, **Notes**, with a box at the bottom for
adding one.

**The key things you can do here.** Add an enquiry, edit it, move it through the stages,
add follow-up tasks with dates, write notes after a conversation, approve a family, and
edit the email wording sent at each stage through **Email Templates**.

**How it connects to the rest of the portal.** Here is the most important thing in this
section, and it is worth planning your process around:

**An enquiry does not automatically become a child.** The enquiry list and the **Children**
page are separate. Moving a family to **Accepted**, or all the way to **Registered**, does
**not** create a child record. When a family is joining, somebody opens the **Children**
page and enters the child properly — name, birth date, parent, health details, pickup
people, and the registration that puts them in a room.

So every family who joins is entered twice: once as an enquiry, once as a child. Build that
handover into your routine — a standing rule such as "when a lead reaches Accepted, create
the child record the same day" prevents a family being welcomed on Monday and missing from
the register.

Each enquiry must name a **Referral** and a **Source**, both maintained on the **Referrals
& Sources** page. Enquiry figures also feed the **CRM** tab of the **Live Dashboard**.

**Worth knowing.** **The status badge on the lead row is the authoritative stage.** Some
stage names appear differently on the **Live Dashboard** and in the menu you use to move an
enquiry along — most noticeably around the tour stages, where the dashboard and this page
use different words for the same point. When you need to know where a family genuinely
stands, read the badge here. Use the dashboard for the monthly shape, use this page for the
individual answer.

One consequence follows from that, and it saves confusion: if you move an enquiry using a
menu option and the badge afterwards displays different wording, the enquiry has moved
correctly. The stage is right; the two screens simply name it differently. You have not done
anything wrong.

**A tour is recorded as a stage plus its follow-up task.** There is no separate tour booking
screen and no calendar slot for it. Set the stage, and capture the date and time in the
task — which is exactly why the task date is worth filling in properly.

**Changing a stage creates a follow-up task and asks for a reminder date.** Treat that
prompt as useful rather than as an obstacle: it is your prompt to decide when you will next
contact the family. But **nothing in the portal will chase you on that date** — no alert, no
notification. Your own diary, or a daily habit of opening this page and reading the
**Tasks** panel, is what makes the reminder work.

Two stages have small practical quirks. **Registered** is set by adding a task rather than
from the badge menu, so to move a family there, open the enquiry, press **+** in the Tasks
column, and set the status there. And the **Approve** button in the detail header sets the
family to accepted directly without creating a task; once pressed it reads **Approved** and
locks, which you can undo by moving the stage from the badge menu.

Read stages by their words rather than their colours. Only one stage is red, and two rather
different outcomes share a similar blue, so the word on the badge is the reliable signal.

Enquiries stay in the list after they conclude, whether the family joined or not. There is
no status filter, so the list grows over time — use the search to find a specific family.

There is a useful distinction between two closing stages: **Lost** means the family
withdrew or went elsewhere, **Rejected** means the nursery declined. Record the reason as a
**Note** or in the comment box when you change the stage, so the history explains itself
later.

### Referrals & Sources

**Open this area:** [Referrals & Sources](https://peek.peek-a-boo.app/app/crm/referrals-sources)

**What this area is for.** The two lookup lists that record where enquiries come from.

**Who normally uses it.** The system administrator. Like Roles & Access, this page is
restricted, so most staff will not see it.

**What you will see.** The page is headed **Referrals & Communication Sources**. Across the
top are five ranking cards showing the top referrals by number of enquiries, numbered **#1**
to **#5**. Below, two panels side by side: **Referrals** on the left, each row numbered and
showing its name and an amber type pill, and **Communication Sources** on the right.

**The key things you can do here.** Add, edit and remove entries in either list.

**How it connects to the rest of the portal.** These two lists fill required fields on every
enquiry. **Referral** answers *how did the family hear about us?* — a campaign, a sign, a
recommendation. **Source** answers *which channel did the enquiry arrive through?* Both are
required on every lead.

That produces a connection worth flagging in advance: because both lists are required and
this page is restricted, **admissions staff cannot add a missing option themselves.** If a
registrar cannot save an enquiry because the right referral is not in the list, they need an
administrator to add it.

The sensible answer is to set both lists up properly at the start, with the office team
involved, and add to them promptly when a new channel appears.

**Worth knowing.** **The lists start empty.** There is no default set — what your nursery
has is what your administrators created.

The ranking cards at the top are genuinely useful: they show which referral sources are
actually producing enquiries, which is the closest thing the portal offers to marketing
feedback. They count enquiries, not enrolments.

The small grip handle at the end of each row is decorative. The list order is fixed and
cannot be dragged.

## A short closing checklist

If you remember nothing else from this guide, remember these.

1. **Create the child, then register the child.** Without a registration they appear in no
   register, no report and no class list.
2. **Mark the register on the day.** Both attendance pages are today-only, and past dates
   cannot be corrected. **Attendance Reports** is for looking up history, not fixing it.
3. **Publish and email are different.** Publishing finishes a daily report; **Publish &
   Email** sends it to the family.
4. **Record illness, injury and temperature in the Note to parents box, and tell the room
   lead in person.** Ongoing conditions belong in the child's **Health** section too.
5. **An enquiry does not become a child.** Somebody creates the child record by hand when a
   family joins.
6. **My Center is your nursery. Children Attendance is not Staff Attendance.** The two pairs
   that confuse everyone.
7. **Deactivate rather than delete.** It keeps the history and it can be undone.
8. **On Balance by Class, grey means owing and red means in credit.**
9. **The Leads page is the authority on an enquiry's stage**, not the dashboard.
10. **Only the dashboard refreshes itself.** Everywhere else, reload the page to see a
    colleague's changes.
